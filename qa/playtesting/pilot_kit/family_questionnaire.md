# Family questionnaire (DEC-003)

Asked straight after playing. Same order for everyone: **open questions first, then the one about creatures treating you
differently, then the ratings.** That way the later questions can't put ideas into the earlier answers. Never say "remember" or
"memory" before the last question is answered.

Good practice used here:
- **Ages 4–7: asked aloud** by the parent, who writes the answers down word for word. Short, concrete words. A **5-face scale**
  instead of numbers, and one question about whether they'd play again (children this age tend to pick the happiest face for
  everything, so the "again" question and what they say count more than the faces).
- **Ages 8–14:** read it themselves, or with help. Faces scale. Writing is optional; saying it aloud is fine.
- **Adults:** the full questionnaire in `questionnaire.md`.
- **Don't help with the answer.** If the child is stuck: "Anything is fine" or "Can you show me in the game?" Never suggest one.
- **Accept "I don't know"**, and stop if the child is tired. Five minutes at most.

## Ages 4–7 (parent asks aloud)
1. **"What happened in the game?"**  *(write down what they say)*
2. **"Did an animal do something funny or surprising? What did it do?"**
3. **"Was any animal nicer to you, or more scared of you, than before? Why do you think so?"**

## Writing it down
The faces are scored 1–5 (😢 = 1 … 😄 = 5). Note them under the session number together with the answers, in the family's own
notes. The report tool's `responses.csv` uses a 1–7 scale for the adult test, so family answers are **not** put into it. They
are read as they are.
